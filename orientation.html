To all volunteers: client confidentiality is vital

Please do not divulge, under any circumstances, the identity of any client coming to the pantry. Do not access or discuss client information with anyone.

Client information books store important, confidential information. Other than the desk person or Shift Supervisor accessing that information for Food Pantry operational purposes, no one should be reading or looking up client information.

Anyone asking for information about another client should be directed to the Shift Supervisor, who will take appropriate measures.

Mission & Overview

Our mission

To provide emergency food assistance to those in need in our community.

Community

Our community is primarily those living in the Quakertown School District area and other portions of Upper Bucks County. Food is distributed to those who request help — we provide a supplemental food supply intended to supplement a household's grocery budget.

Sources for food

  • TEFAP — The Emergency Food Assistance Program (federal government)
  • SFPP — State Food Purchase Program (state grant). Both government programs come through the Bucks County Opportunity Council (BCOC).
  • Food purchased from Philabundance and area grocery stores
  • Regular contributions of baked goods and other items from local grocery stores
  • Food donations collected in local schools, businesses, churches, groups, families, and individuals
  • Funds received from private donors, churches, and area businesses
The pantry is run solely by volunteers — there are no paid positions.
Getting Started

Pantry facts

  • We give out about 11,000 lbs of food each week — much more at the holidays
  • Families range from 1 person up to 14
  • We generally serve 30 to 50 families a day (more at the holidays)
  • The Management Team creates a weekly menu that strives to provide 3 meals a day for 3 days when donations permit
  • The size of the family determines the amount of food given

Serving clients

Clients must complete a government affidavit of need once a year, and provide a current ID showing they live in the Quakertown School District area each time they visit the pantry.

Our Story

Pantry history

In the 1980s, the Bucks County Opportunity Council (BCOC) kept a small supply of food in their offices for clients in immediate need. BCOC approached area churches for help meeting the needs of the poor, and the churches of the Quakertown Ministerium — 12 houses of worship — agreed to support the effort.

In 2004, the Ministerium agreed to take over running the food pantry. It was formally named the Quakertown Food Pantry, and hours expanded greatly — from one day a month to five days a week.

In 2008, the pantry became a 501(c)(3), independent from the Ministerium, with every board member taking an active role in operations. In 2010, the pantry moved to its current home — the maintenance wood shop offered by the Quakertown Borough.

Today we pick up food most days of the week from the two Giant supermarkets, Walmart, and Target. Many businesses, schools, churches, organizations, and individuals donate food and funds, and we receive annual federal and state grants. QFP is a proud member of Philabundance and BCOC.

Annual Training Required

A quick guide to civil rights

U.S. Department of Agriculture, Food and Nutrition Service. To ensure equal access to our programs, participating entities must:

1. Let people know how to apply and how to file a complaint

  • Place the "And Justice For All" poster where it can easily be seen
  • Use the Nondiscrimination Statement on all materials and websites that mention FNS Programs
  • Notify community groups that the program is available; provide information when requested
  • Schedule hours of operation according to the needs of the community

2. Identify and accommodate language needs

  • Find out what languages are spoken in your service area
  • Make sure everyone knows what to do when a Limited English Proficient client needs help
  • Provide translated material where many people speak another language; use certified interpreters when needed
  • Do not ask clients to bring their own interpreters

3. Accommodate persons with disabilities

  • Allow persons with disabilities to use predesignated proxies to pick up food packages
  • Provide clients with hearing and vision impairments the accommodations they need

4. Data on race and ethnicity (CSFP)

  • Explain that collecting the data is required by law, will not affect eligibility, and helps prevent discrimination
  • You must ask clients to self-identify; clients may identify more than one race

5. If a client reports being treated differently

  • Try to resolve issues as quickly as possible
  • Refer the client to the Civil Rights contact or the address on the poster
  • Offer the client a civil rights complaint form

Holidays & inclement weather

Inclement weather

QFP follows the Quakertown School District closing schedule — if the schools are closed, the pantry is closed. This is posted on the Facebook page.

  • Volunteers use their discretion about volunteering at their shift
  • The Shift Supervisor on duty makes the call about road and travel conditions — the safety of our volunteers and clients is always the first priority
  • For all shifts, contact your Shift Supervisor ASAP about whether you plan to be at the pantry, so they can determine if there are enough volunteers and if conditions are safe to open
  • Reach out to the Management Team or a Board Member if you are uncertain

Policy statements & approval dates

Policy 01
Wish List
Approved July 13, 2017 · Revised 2/11/2021 · Revised 11/14/2024

Menu items (cans, packaged goods, small-quantity items) will only be added to the Wish List by the Management Team. Dairy or refrigerated items, except cheese, will be added by Shift Supervisors.

  • Menu items stay on the Wish List until we run out or the Management Team removes them (no member removes an item without consulting 2 others)
  • Dairy/refrigerated items should be moved out quickly; the Shift Supervisor checks for these at the beginning of each shift
Policy 02
Out of Date Items
Approved July 13, 2017 · Revised 11/14/2024

Following Feeding America/Philabundance guidelines, any food past the 1-year expiration date shall not be put on the menu.

  • Items with questionable dates go in a designated area (one for canned/boxed, one for frozen) for Management Team review at the weekly meeting
  • Large quantities with questionable dates go on the table outside the kitchen with a note asking the Management Team to review
Policy 03
Give Away
Approved July 13, 2017 · Revised 11/14/2024

Canned/boxed items past 1 year from expiration, dented cans, and overflow (produce, bread, candy, etc.) go in the large cart for clients to take at their own discretion.

  • Shift Supervisors or the Management Team place odd items in the large cart
  • During summer/hot weather, shifts throw away any items remaining in the cart at the end of their shift
Policy 04
Repacking of Food Items
Approved 5-0

To comply with Philabundance and Serv-Safe guidelines, the pantry will no longer repack any food items except:

  • Individually wrapped tea bags
  • Individually wrapped sugar & sweetener
  • Keurig K cups
  • Loose produce
  • Dog & cat food

No loose bread items may be repacked.

Policy 05
Homemade Food
Approved August 9, 2017

Any homemade canned, frozen, or prepared food products cannot be accepted under any circumstances. The pantry only accepts commercially canned, frozen, or prepared food items, and a copy of the organization's current Serv-Safe Certification must be on file.

Policy 06
Organizational Prepared Food
Board approved 5-0 · August 9, 2017

Food prepared by organizations (frozen or prepared) is accepted only if:

  • A copy of the organization's current Serv-Safe Certification is on file at the pantry
  • The food item has a label listing all ingredients (for Philabundance compliance)
Policy 07
Fresh Produce
Approved August 9, 2017

Produce is given out at each shift following "most perishable first." If the shift supervisor judges produce won't last until the next shift, give extra to each client.

  • Most perishable: grapes, berries, tomatoes, cut vegetables & fruits, lettuce, spinach, kale, peppers, cucumbers, peaches, bananas
  • Least perishable (whole, not cut): potatoes, carrots, onions, cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, apples, oranges, squash
  • Trash bags are not safe for food and may affect a client's health
Policy 08
Frozen Food
Approved October 11, 2017

Following Feeding America/Philabundance guidelines, any frozen food past the 1-year expiration date shall not be put on the menu.

  • The Management Team documents the date all frozen food is received and places like items in the same bin
  • The Management Team monitors the freezers to ensure frozen food is put on the menu in a timely manner and before the 1-year expiration date
Policy 09
Shelving & Rotation of Food
Approved by the Board April 11, 2018

Rotation order: Federal food is given out first, then State food, then donated food starting with the oldest date (Philabundance is considered donated food). Items are shelved in designated shopping areas with extra storage above or below.

Policy 10
Out of Area Clients
Approved February 7, 2018 · Clarification Oct 25, 2018 · Revised Nov 13, 2024

Families outside QFP's service area (Upper Bucks County and the Quakertown School District) are advised of the nearest pantry. Only 1 pre-packed bag (canned & dry good) per family — not per person — plus bread & desserts.

  • Any client without proper ID is given a pre-packed bag after the 3rd time
  • In an emergency, pre-packed bags may be given
  • Out-of-town families may also receive bread and dessert if available — no meat is given
  • They must fill out all paperwork as a new client and be marked as referred to the proper pantry
Policy 11
Donated Eggs
Approved by the Board May 13, 2020

Donated eggs must be in dozen or dozen-and-a-half cartons. Per Philabundance and Serv-Safe guidelines the pantry cannot repack any food items (see Policy 04). Always check with the Management Team or in the egg refrigerator before accepting donated eggs.

Policy 12
Flu and Contagious Disease
Approved March 11, 2020 · Revised November 30, 2020

This policy protects volunteers and clients during a pandemic, especially those at high risk. Protocols cover: (A) volunteer and client safety, (B) cleaning and disinfecting, and (C) procedures upon discovery that the pantry has been exposed to a probable or confirmed case. See the full policy for complete details.

Policy 13
Volunteer Training
Approved November 11, 2015 · Amended May 4, 2016

Anyone who comes into the pantry to volunteer must go through a training program. A student needing only a few hours for a school service project will receive on-the-job training, be paired with a trained volunteer, and will not work alone.

Policy 14
Volunteer Age Requirements
Approved August 3, 2016
  • Volunteers 15 and under must be accompanied by a parent or guardian
  • Volunteers 16 and above can work without a parent/guardian
  • Volunteers must be 21 to work at the desk
  • Youth groups must have at least 2 adult supervisors from that group present
Policy 15
Pantry Clients as Volunteers
Approved October 8, 2019

The pantry will only accept volunteers who are not currently clients, due to conflicts and confidentiality risks.

  • Community members who donate food expect it to go to clients, not volunteers
  • If a volunteer needs food, they must contact the board and may need to temporarily suspend volunteering and sign up as a client
Policy 16
Court Ordered Service Hours
Approved May 4, 2016 · Amended April 10, 2019
  • Anyone with court-ordered community service will not be accepted as a volunteer
  • Juveniles referred to the Youth Aid Panel are accepted — applicants must email the pantry to be considered; all juveniles under 16 must be accompanied by a parent
Policy 17
Meetings by Outside Groups
Approved February 13, 2019

Use of the pantry for meetings is limited to groups or individuals affiliated with the pantry. Unless approved by the Board, outside groups may not use the premises.

  • Concerns include client/volunteer confidentiality, liability, and use of utilities
  • Groups like the Crop Walk and Lion's Club are grandfathered in
  • Affiliated groups (Quakertown Outreach — Joanne's group, Code Blue) may continue
Policy 18
Food Distribution
Approved by the Board November 13, 2024

The pantry is a source of food for all those in need who approach us. Any volunteer providing food must:

  • Ensure client paperwork is completed
  • Provide only what is on the menu that week, according to family size
  • Document the distribution on the sign-in sheet and client book
Policy 19
Meat Portion Size
Approved at Board Meeting October 8, 2025

Weekly menus are based on estimated portion sizes relative to household size. Meats are placed in bins marked for households of 1–2 and 3+. Additional meats are in the rolling cart in the walk-in freezer.

  • Bone-in meat: ½ lb (8 oz) per person
  • Boneless/ground meat: ⅓ lb (6 oz) per person
  • Individual cuts (small pork chops, chicken thighs, small steaks): 1 per person. Larger cuts (extra-large chicken breasts, large/thick steaks): serve 2 people per cut.
  • Frozen meat-based meals (Banquet, Lean Cuisine, etc.): households of 1–2 people
  • Fish sticks: 1 box serves 1–2 people
  • Fish fillets (10 per box): 1 box serves 3–5 people
  • Small whole chickens: serve 2–3 people · Turkeys: serve 4+ people
  • Hot dogs: 2 per person — 1–4 people: 1 pack of 8; 5–8 people: 2 packs; 9+ people: 3 packs
Do not give several small portions to one large household.
