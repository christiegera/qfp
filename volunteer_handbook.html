Welcome to the team

Thank you for volunteering with the Quakertown Food Pantry. Every shift you work, every bag you pack, and every client you greet makes a real difference for families in our community.

This handbook covers everything you need to know — from your first day through daily operations, policies, and who to call when you have a question. Keep it handy, and don't hesitate to reach out if anything's unclear.

We're glad you're here.

— The QFP Board of Directors
Quick Reference

Produce Priority

  • First: Grapes, berries, tomatoes, cut veggies, lettuce, spinach, bananas
  • Last: Potatoes, carrots, onions, cabbage, apples, oranges, squash
  • If it won't last until next shift, give extra to each client

Food Rotation Order

  • 1. Federal food (TEFAP) first
  • 2. State food (SFPP) second
  • 3. Donated food, oldest date first
  • Philabundance = donated food

We Don't Accept

  • Homemade/home-canned food
  • Food past 1 year from expiration
  • Non-food items (clothing, toys, etc.)
  • Glass jars (safety hazard)
  • Loose bread for repacking
Mission & Overview

Our mission

To provide emergency food assistance to those in need in our community.

Community

Our community is primarily those living in the Quakertown Community School District area and other portions of Upper Bucks County. Food is distributed to those who request help — we provide supplemental food to help supplement a household's grocery budget.

Food sources

  • TEFAP — The Emergency Food Assistance Program (federal)
  • SFPP — State Food Purchase Program via Bucks County Opportunity Council
  • Food purchased from Philabundance and area grocery stores
  • Regular donations from local schools, businesses, churches, and individuals
  • Funds from private donors, churches, and area businesses
100% Volunteer-Run — there are no paid positions.

Key facts

  • 20–60 families served each day
  • Maximum household size: 14
  • Open 5 days per week
  • Each visit provides 3 days of meals
  • Clients may visit once per week
  • Clients complete a government affidavit of need annually and show current ID proving Quakertown School District residency at each visit
Our Story

Pantry history

For years, a single volunteer from the local Methodist church kept a small food supply and opened the doors once a month — or whenever a BCOC social worker needed food for a client.

When he moved to Florida in 2004, the Quakertown Ministerium — 12 houses of worship — stepped in to take over and expand the effort, giving it a name: the Quakertown Food Pantry. Hours expanded to five days a week.

In 2008, the pantry became an independent 501(c)(3), with every board member taking an active role in operations. In 2010, we moved to the maintenance wood shop offered by the Quakertown Borough at 101 N. Third Street — our home to this day.

In 2019, a local Girl Scout troop ran a toiletry drive for their Bronze Award, which sparked our Amazon Wish List. In 2020, we added online donations via PayPal and Venmo.

Today we pick up food from two Giant supermarkets, Walmart, and Target, and we're proud members of Philabundance and BCOC.

Getting Started

Volunteer requirements

Orientation

All volunteers must attend an orientation session before their first shift. Students with only a few hours for school service projects will receive on-the-job training and be paired with a trained volunteer — they will not work alone.

Age requirements

  • Volunteers 15 and under must be accompanied by a parent or guardian
  • Volunteers 16 and above can work without a parent/guardian
  • Must be 21+ to work at the front desk or in the office
  • Youth groups must have at least 2 adult supervisors present

Important policies

  • We do not accept volunteers with court-ordered community service (juveniles referred to the Youth Aid Panel are accepted — email us first)
  • Current pantry clients cannot volunteer due to confidentiality concerns. If a volunteer needs food, contact the board to discuss options.
Annual Training Required

Civil rights quick guide

U.S. Department of Agriculture, Food & Nutrition Service (FNS Instruction 113). All volunteers must review this guide each year.

1. Let people know how to apply and how to file a complaint

  • Place the "And Justice For All" poster where it can easily be seen
  • Use the Nondiscrimination Statement on all materials and websites that mention FNS Programs
  • Notify community groups that the program is available
  • Schedule hours of operation according to the needs of the community

2. Identify and accommodate language needs

  • Find out what languages are spoken in your service area
  • Make sure everyone knows what to do when a Limited English Proficient client needs help
  • Provide translated material if there are many people who speak another language
  • Do not ask clients to bring their own interpreters

3. Accommodate persons with disabilities

  • Allow persons with disabilities to use predesignated proxies to pick up food packages
  • Provide clients with hearing and vision impairments the accommodations they need

4. Data collection (CSFP)

  • Explain to clients that collecting race and ethnicity data is required by law and will not affect eligibility
  • You must ask clients to self-identify; clients may identify more than one race

5. If a client reports being treated differently

  • Try to resolve issues as quickly as possible
  • Refer the client to the Civil Rights contact or address on the poster
  • Offer the client a civil rights complaint form

For more information: FNS MARO OCR at (609) 259-5061/5123

Board

  • Update policies
  • Approve financial expenditures (check signing by President and VP)
  • Informational talks and pantry tours as requested
  • Handle client issues and emergencies
  • Call board meetings
  • Communicate with the communications team for website/Facebook updates
  • Send monthly emails to shift supervisors about board decisions and schedule changes
  • Answer questions from donors about how donations are used

Treasurer

  • Financial donations and paying bills
  • Bookkeeping and monthly reporting

Secretary

  • Board minutes and thank-you letters to donors
  • Set up schedule for pantry holidays and post signs
  • Maintain list of board members, positions, and phone numbers

Management Team

  • Day-to-day operations, food delivery/pickups, stocking
  • Create menus and order/buy food
  • Manage freezer operations
  • BCOC and Philabundance liaison, grant coordination
  • Maintain packing schedules for client bags and bonus bags
  • Maintain GAH driver and helper schedules
  • Pantry cleaning coordination

  • Welcome new volunteers (orientation)
  • Answer emails about volunteering
  • Set up training with team leads
  • Maintain shift schedules, volunteer roster, and supervisor contact list
  • Handle volunteer issues

  • Coordinate scheduling and volunteers
  • Date checking and shelving large donations

Office Team

  • Retrieve phone messages

  • Enter and file pantry registration forms
  • Complete Philabundance weekly reports
  • Maintain computer, printer, and office supplies
  • Create/print forms and signs as needed
  • Maintain outside bulletin boards

Communications Team

  • Community communications and advertising
  • Event coordination

Shift Supervisors

  • Open and close the pantry during a shift

Shift Volunteers

  • Serve clients, stock shelves, receive donations

Calling out

If you can't make your scheduled shift, contact your Shift Supervisor as soon as possible. Their contact information is in the Shift Schedule section of this handbook.

If you can't reach your supervisor, contact a member of the Management Team or a Board Member.

The earlier you let us know, the easier it is to find coverage. Even a quick text or email helps.

Inclement weather

The pantry follows the Quakertown Community School District — if schools close, the pantry closes.

  • Volunteers use their own discretion on travel safety
  • Contact your Shift Supervisor ASAP
  • Shift Supervisor makes the final call on opening
  • Reach out to Management or a Board Member if uncertain
Closure notices are posted on our Facebook page.

Policy statements

Policy 01
Wish List Policy
Approved July 13, 2017 · Revised 11/14/2024

Menu items (cans, packaged goods, small quantity items) will only be added to the Wish List by the Management Team. Dairy or refrigerated items, except cheese, will be added by Shift Supervisors.

  • Menu items stay on the Wish List until we run out or the Management Team removes them (requires consulting 2 other members)
  • Dairy/refrigerated items should be moved out quickly; Shift Supervisor checks at the beginning of each shift
Policy 02
Out of Date Items
Approved July 13, 2017 · Revised 11/14/2024

Any food past the 1-year expiration date shall not be put on the menu (per Feeding America/Philabundance guidelines).

  • Items with questionable dates go in a designated area for Management Team review
  • Large quantities with questionable dates: place on the table outside the kitchen with a note
Policy 03
Front Give Away Shelf
Approved July 13, 2017

Canned/boxed items past 1 year from expiration, dented cans, and overflow (produce, bread, candy) go on the Front Give Away Shelf for clients to take at their discretion.

  • During summer/hot weather, shifts throw away remaining items at end of shift
Policy 04
Repacking of Food Items
Board Approved

The pantry will no longer repack any food items except:

  • Individually wrapped tea bags, sugar & sweetener, Keurig K cups
  • Loose produce
  • Dog & cat food
  • No loose bread items may be repacked
Policy 05
Homemade Food
Approved August 9, 2017

Any homemade canned, frozen, or prepared food products cannot be accepted under any circumstances. Only commercially produced items with a current Serv-Safe Certification on file.

Policy 06
Organizational Prepared Food
Approved August 9, 2017

Food prepared by organizations accepted only if:

  • A copy of the organization's current Serv-Safe Certification is on file
  • The food item has a label listing all ingredients
Policy 07
Fresh Produce
Approved August 9, 2017

Give out produce at each shift following "most perishable first." If produce won't last until the next shift, give out extra to each client.

  • Most perishable: Grapes, berries, tomatoes, cut vegetables & fruits, lettuce, spinach, kale, peppers, cucumbers, peaches, bananas
  • Least perishable: Potatoes, carrots, onions, cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, apples, oranges, squash
  • Trash bags are not safe for food per Feeding America/Philabundance guidelines
Policy 08
Frozen Food
Approved October 11, 2017

Frozen food past the 1-year expiration date shall not be put on the menu. Management Team documents the date all frozen food is received and monitors freezers to ensure timely use.

Policy 09
Non-Food Items
Approved October 11, 2017

The pantry can no longer accept or store non-food items, except paper products and personal items used for pantry operations. No clothing, toys, electronics, dishes, etc.

Policy 11
Shelving & Rotation of Food
Approved April 11, 2018

Rotation order: Federal food first, then State food, then donated food starting with the oldest date. Philabundance is considered donated food. Items are shelved in designated shopping areas with extra storage above or below.

Policy 12
Pre-Packed Bag / Out of Area Clients
Approved February 7, 2018 · Revised November 13, 2024

Out-of-area families will be advised of the nearest pantry and given 1 pre-packed bag (canned & dry goods) per family plus bread & desserts.

  • Clients without proper ID get a pre-packed bag after the 3rd time
  • Out-of-town families are not given Federal/State food or meats
  • Must fill out all paperwork and be marked as referred to the proper pantry
Policy 13
Donated Eggs
Approved May 13, 2020

Donated eggs must be in dozen or dozen-and-a-half cartons. Cannot repack eggs. Always check with the management team or in the egg refrigerator before accepting.

Policy 14
Flu and Contagious Disease
Approved March 11, 2020 · Revised November 30, 2020

Protocols cover volunteer and client safety, cleaning and disinfecting, and procedures upon exposure to a confirmed case. See the full policy document for complete details.

Policy 20
Food Distribution Statement
Approved November 13, 2024

Any volunteer providing food must adhere to these guidelines:

  • Client paperwork must be completed
  • Provide only what is on the menu that week, according to family size
  • Document the distribution on the sign-in sheet and client book
